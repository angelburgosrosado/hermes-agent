## 1. Purpose & Goals

Leafywell product descriptions must:

- Explain what the product **supports**, in calm, realistic terms.  
- Make it easy for customers to decide if it fits their needs.  
- Be compliant with supplement marketing rules: no disease claims, no guarantees [web:50][web:53].  
- Reflect Leafywell’s voice: calm, clear, nature-based, science-aware.  


## 2. Default Structure

Every product description should follow this structure (sections can be merged if needed):

1. Hero block (name, short subtitle, 1–2 benefit bullets).  
2. “What it supports” (outcomes in plain language).  
3. “How it works” (key ingredients + simple explanations).  
4. “How to use it” (dose, timing, routine).  
5. “What to expect” (realistic expectations and timeframe).  
6. “What’s inside” (ingredients overview; detailed list can be separate).  
7. “Who it’s for / not for” (high-level guidance + doctor disclaimer).  
8. FAQ section (3–7 questions).  

Do NOT add sections that imply treatment, cure, or prevention of disease [web:50][web:53].  


## 3. Hero Block Rules

### 3.1 Name and subtitle

- Use the exact product name from BRAND.md terminology.  
- Subtitle: 1 short line that states the primary support area, not a claim.  

Examples (ON-BRAND):

- “Leafywell Sleep & Relax – nightly support for winding down.”  
- “Leafywell Daily Metabolic Support – simple help for steady energy and metabolism.”  

OFF-BRAND examples:

- “Leafywell Sleep & Relax – cures insomnia fast!”  
- “Daily Metabolic Support – melt fat effortlessly.”  

Never use “cure,” “treat,” “prevent,” “reverse,” or similar verbs [web:50][web:53].  

### 3.2 Benefit bullets

- 2–4 bullets max.  
- Each bullet begins with a verb like “Supports,” “Helps,” or “Promotes.”  
- Focus on normal-function support in healthy people, not disease treatment [web:50][web:53].  

Example bullets (ON-BRAND):

- “Supports a calm, relaxed evening routine.”  
- “Helps you feel more steady, day to day.”  
- “Supports a healthy response to everyday stress.”  


## 4. “What It Supports” Section

### 4.1 Intent

Explain, in plain language, what areas of life or health this product is designed to support.

Guidelines:

- Focus on experiences: energy, focus, calm, sleep quality, appetite awareness, general metabolic support.  
- Emphasize “support” and “help,” not “fix” or “heal.”  
- Avoid naming specific diseases or diagnostic labels [web:50][web:53].  

Example paragraph (ON-BRAND):

> “Daily Metabolic Support is for people who want steadier energy and more consistent daily rhythms. It’s designed to support a healthy metabolism, help you feel more ‘even’ through the day, and make it easier to stick to the habits that matter.”  

OFF-BRAND:

> “This formula reverses metabolic syndrome and fixes your hormones.”  


## 5. “How It Works” Section

### 5.1 Ingredient explanation

For each key ingredient or blend:

- Name it.  
- State what it supports, in everyday language.  
- If referencing research, keep it general and avoid over-specific promises; do not cite individual studies unless they are in the Proof Library [web:45][web:50][web:53].  

Example (ON-BRAND):

> “Magnesium glycinate supports relaxation and muscle comfort, which can make it easier to unwind at night.”  

OFF-BRAND:

> “Magnesium glycinate treats anxiety and eliminates muscle pain.”  

### 5.2 Structure-function style

Use structure-function phrasing: “supports,” “helps maintain,” “promotes,” “assists with,” “encourages,” “helps you feel,” etc. [web:50][web:53].  

Forbidden framing:

- “prevents,” “cures,” “treats,” “reverses,” “heals” (for diseases or conditions).  


## 6. “How to Use It” Section

### 6.1 Dosing

Always include:

- How many units (e.g., gummies, capsules, droppers).  
- How often (per day).  
- When (time of day or with meals, if relevant).  
- Basic routine guidance (e.g., “Take at the same time each day”).  

Example (ON-BRAND):

> “Take two gummies once daily with a glass of water, preferably with food. For those new to supplements, starting with one gummy for the first week can help you see how your body responds.”  

### 6.2 Safety and disclaimers

Always include some version of:

> “If you have a medical condition, are taking medications, are pregnant or nursing, talk to your healthcare provider before use.”  

Never give individual medical advice.  


## 7. “What to Expect” Section

### 7.1 Set realistic expectations

- Be honest about timeframes (e.g., “Many people notice…in X–Y days/weeks,” if supported by your internal data).  
- Acknowledge variation: not everyone feels the same effect, at the same time.  

Example (ON-BRAND):

> “Some people notice changes in how they feel within the first 1–2 weeks. For others, differences show up more gradually over a month as they stay consistent with their routine.”  

Never promise:

- “You will feel X within 24 hours.”  
- “Guaranteed results.”  


## 8. “What’s Inside” Section

### 8.1 Ingredients overview

- Summarize key ingredients and their roles (no disease claims).  
- Mention clean-label choices (vegan, non-GMO, etc.) when true.  

Example:

> “You’ll find a blend of magnesium glycinate, L-theanine, and calming botanicals, all selected to support relaxation without leaving you groggy.”  

### 8.2 Label details

If providing full Supplement Facts, keep formatting consistent but do NOT invent numbers. Pull actual values from your product data.  


## 9. “Who It’s For / Not For”

### 9.1 Who it’s for

Use a short list (3–5 bullets) describing ideal use cases.

Example:

- “People who want a calmer evening routine.”  
- “Adults aiming to support healthy metabolic function.”  

### 9.2 Who it’s not for

- “Children, unless directed by a healthcare provider.”  
- “People with specific medical conditions where these ingredients may be contraindicated (consult your provider).”  

Always close with a doctor disclaimer for edge cases.  


## 10. FAQ Section

Each product page should include 3–7 FAQs.

Common question types:

- “When should I take this?”  
- “Can I take this with other Leafywell products?”  
- “How long should I use this?”  
- “What if I don’t notice anything?”  
- Shipping / returns / subscription basics.  

Rules:

- Answers must be short, clear, and avoid personal medical advice.  
- When in doubt, guide customers to speak with a healthcare provider.  


## 11. SEO & Readability Rules

- Focus each product page on 1–2 primary keyphrases (e.g., “metabolic support supplement,” “sleep support gummies”), used naturally in headings and copy [web:45][web:51][web:54].  
- Use H2/H3 headings that reflect how users search (e.g., “How to use [Product]”).  
- Short paragraphs and bullets for scanning.  
- No keyword stuffing; user clarity comes first.  


## 12. Voice Examples (for Product Pages)

### 12.1 On-brand example snippet

> “Leafywell Sleep & Relax is for evenings when your mind doesn’t get the memo that it’s time to wind down. With a gentle blend of magnesium, L-theanine, and calming botanicals, it’s designed to support relaxation so you can move into your nighttime routine feeling more settled.”  

### 12.2 Off-brand example snippet

> “Leafywell Sleep & Relax knocks you out fast and cures insomnia, so you finally sleep like a baby again—guaranteed.”  

Never generate copy that resembles the off-brand example.
