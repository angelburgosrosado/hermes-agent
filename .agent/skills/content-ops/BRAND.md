## 1. Identity & Context

**Brand name:** Leafywell
**Tagline:** Feel more like yourself, every day.
**One-sentence brand promise:** Nature-based supplements that support how you feel day to day — without overpromising.
**Mission statement:** To make honest, nature-informed supplements that help real people feel steadier, calmer, and more like themselves — without the hype.

**Industry / category:** Dietary supplements / wellness
**Business model:** DTC e-commerce
**Primary geography / markets:** United States
**Price position:** Mid-market to premium

**Core company narrative:**
Most supplement brands overpromise and underdeliver, leaving customers skeptical and confused. Leafywell was built on a different premise: honest, nature-informed formulas that support the body's normal functions — no miracle claims, no jargon walls. We make it easy for health-curious adults to find something that fits their routine and actually trust what's on the label.


## 2. Audience

### 2.1 Primary audience

**Who they are:** Health-conscious adults (25–55), primarily women, who are proactive about their wellness but skeptical of hype and miracle claims.

**What they know:** They understand that supplements support, not fix. They read labels. They've been burned by products that overpromised. They know basics like magnesium and melatonin but may not know clinical nuances.

**What they want most:** To feel more steady, calm, and consistent in their daily energy and sleep — without complicated routines or risky ingredients.

**Top 5 pain points:**
1. Trouble winding down at night / inconsistent sleep quality
2. Low or uneven energy through the day
3. Difficulty managing everyday stress without feeling medicated
4. Overwhelm from too many products, confusing labels, and conflicting claims
5. Skepticism — have tried supplements before that did nothing

**Top 5 objections / fears:**
1. "Will this actually work for me?"
2. "Are the ingredients safe / what's actually in this?"
3. "Is this just another overhyped product?"
4. "I don't want to be on something forever."
5. "What if I'm pregnant / on medication?"

### 2.2 Secondary audience

**Who they are:** Health-focused gift buyers (family members purchasing for a parent or partner)
**How they differ:** Less ingredient-savvy; lead with ease-of-use and trust signals
**What matters most to them:** Safety, simplicity, and brand credibility

## 5. Voice & Tone

### 5.1 Personality

We sound like a person who is:
- **Calm** — never urgent, never alarming, never hyped
- **Clear** — plain language, short sentences, no jargon walls
- **Honest** — willing to say "this supports X" instead of "this cures X"
- **Warm** — approachable and human, not clinical or cold
- **Grounded** — nature-informed, evidence-aware, but not preachy about either

**Voice mission statement:**
We speak like a knowledgeable friend who respects your intelligence and your time. We are practical, honest, and focused on how you actually feel — not on what sells.

### 5.2 Voice rules — always do

- Use active voice; keep sentences short.
- Lead with the experience/outcome, then explain the ingredient.
- Use plain language (8th-grade reading level or below).
- Address the reader as "you."
- Use structure-function verbs: supports, helps, promotes, assists with, encourages.
- Acknowledge variation: not everyone responds the same way or at the same pace.
- Set realistic timeframes when referencing results.

### 5.3 Voice rules — never do

- Never use: cure, treat, prevent, reverse, heal, eliminate, fix (in relation to conditions).
- Never use hype phrases: "revolutionary," "game-changing," "miracle," "guaranteed results."
- Never use fear-based language to sell (e.g., "without this you'll suffer from...").
- Never use exclamation marks in support docs or product descriptions.
- Never name a specific disease or diagnostic label (e.g., "insomnia," "anxiety disorder," "metabolic syndrome") as something the product treats.
- Never imply the product replaces medication or medical care.
- Never use emoji in formal content (product pages, support docs, emails).

## 8. Compliance & Legal

- These products are dietary supplements. They are not drugs. Content must never imply otherwise.
- All product claims must use structure-function language, not disease claims (FDA 21 CFR 101.93).
- Include on all product pages: *"These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*
- Include on all product pages: *"If you have a medical condition, are taking medications, or are pregnant or nursing, consult your healthcare provider before use."*
- Do not make guarantees of results. Use: "many people notice," "in our experience," "supports."
- Testimonials must include: first name, last initial, and a descriptor (e.g., "verified customer").
- Any new health or ingredient claim must be reviewed before publishing.
- Pricing: "Prices subject to change."
